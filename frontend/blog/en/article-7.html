Writing Poetry in 32 Languages: The Secrets of AI Multilingual Poetry

Author: Jaekuk Ryu | AI & Poem Editorial Team
Published: April 2, 2026 | Last Updated: April 2, 2026

When I first built AI & Poem, I thought a single Korean poetry generator would be sufficient. But as the service launched, I was surprised to see traffic from countries around the world. A user from Japan requested haiku. Someone in France expected poetry in the alexandrine style. A Spanish-speaking user wanted passionate verse in the spirit of Pablo Neruda. The same genre — "poetry" — yet the expected forms and sensibilities differed dramatically across languages. This realization was what drove me to commit to supporting 32 languages.

Poetry is the pinnacle of linguistic art. Meter, rhythm, metaphor, symbolism — every element that constitutes poetry is deeply rooted in the unique characteristics of its language. So how does AI understand these diverse poetic traditions and create poetry that fits each language? In this article, we explore the differences in poetic DNA across languages, the technical methods AI uses to handle them, and a comparative analysis of poems generated on the same theme in multiple languages.

The Unique DNA of Poetry in Every Language

The world's poetic traditions are remarkably diverse. Korean poetry has a tradition of syllable-counting meters like the 3-4 or 4-4 patterns, and while free verse dominates modern Korean poetry, a distinctive rhythmic sensibility remains crucial. Japanese haiku follows a strict 5-7-5 syllable structure, capturing moments of nature while adhering to unique rules like kireji (cutting words) and kigo (seasonal references). English poetry creates rhythm through the arrangement of stressed syllables, with rich formal traditions like the sonnet (14 lines) and villanelle.

French poetry emphasizes syllable count and rhyme (rime), with the alexandrine (12-syllable line) as its hallmark form. Arabic poetry follows the bahr metrical system, while classical Chinese poetry creates musical harmony through tonal rules called pingze. All these differences are the product of centuries of evolution shaped by each language's phonological structure, grammatical system, and cultural aesthetics.

How AI Distinguishes Haiku from Sonnets

As explained in The Principle of AI Writing Poetry, large language models learn patterns from vast text data. The key to multilingual poetry generation is that this training data includes sufficient literary works from each language. Having learned tens of thousands of Japanese haiku, thousands of English sonnets, and thousands of Korean modern poems, AI statistically grasps the structural features of each form.

For example, when asked to write poetry in Japanese, AI references patterns from Japanese poetry in its training data. The 5-7-5 syllable structure, the use of seasonal words, and the aesthetic of leaving space are naturally reflected. When the same theme arrives in English, conventions of English poetry — iambic pentameter or the enjambment characteristic of free verse — are applied instead.

At AI & Poem, we refined this further by designing language-specific prompts. Rather than simply instructing "write a poem," we include guidelines aligned with each language's poetic traditions and aesthetic expectations. Optimizing these prompts for each of 32 languages was the most time-consuming and experiment-intensive part of developing the service.

Technical Challenges of Multilingual Poetry Generation

Generating poetry in 32 languages is a fundamentally different challenge from translation. When you translate a poem, the original's meter and rhythm are lost. As discussed in Inspiration from the Craft of Famous Poets, a poem's beauty resides not only in its content but deeply in its form. AI & Poem aims not for translation but for independent creation in each language.

Language-Specific Tokenization

Tokenization — the first step in AI text processing — varies dramatically across languages. English separates relatively easily into word units, but Korean, as an agglutinative language, requires consideration of particles and verb conjugations. Japanese mixes kanji, hiragana, and katakana, while Chinese lacks spaces entirely, requiring AI to determine word boundaries independently. Arabic features right-to-left directionality and a connected letter system.

Cultural Differences in Metaphor

Even when expressing the same emotion, the metaphors used differ by culture. In Korean poetry, the moon symbolizes longing and solitude, while in Arabic verse, the moon represents the apex of beauty. In Japanese, cherry blossoms (sakura) simultaneously symbolize beauty and impermanence, a deep symbolic weight they don't carry in Western culture. AI learns these cultural metaphor systems naturally through the literary data of each language.

Comparing AI Poems: Same Theme, Different Sensibilities

Let's compare poems generated by AI on the same theme — "moonlight" — in three different languages.

Korean — Free Verse

Leaning against the window, I look out
The words the moon has spilled
pool on the wooden floor

If I touch them they might scatter
so I hold my breath
and sink into that silence

The Korean poem interprets moonlight through silence and space. By metaphorizing moonlight as "spilled words," it creates a sense of dialogue within silence.

Japanese — Haiku

月光や
水面に揺れる
魚の影

The haiku captures a moment through three layered images — moonlight, water surface, fish shadow — compressing a quiet nightscape into 5-7-5 syllables. Emotions are never stated directly; they are replaced by natural observation, the essential aesthetic of haiku.

English — Sonnet Style

Upon the silent lake the moonlight falls,
A silver thread through darkness softly spun,
It whispers secrets through the ancient walls,
Of all the quiet deeds the night has done.

The English poem uses iambic rhythm and ABAB rhyme scheme to create musical flow. Comparing moonlight to a "silver thread" and personifying the night's actions reflects the Western poetic tradition of rhetorical elaboration.

From the same theme of "moonlight," Korean develops toward inner reflection, Japanese toward capturing a natural moment, and English toward narrative expansion. This is precisely why multilingual poetry generation must be independent creation, not translation.

The Role of Cultural Context in Emotional Expression

Beyond structural differences, each language carries distinct emotional vocabularies that shape how feelings are poeticized. Korean has concepts like "jeong" (정) — a deep, accumulated affection — that have no direct English equivalent. Japanese "mono no aware" (物の哀れ) captures the bittersweet awareness of impermanence. Portuguese "saudade" describes a profound longing for something absent. When AI generates poetry in these languages, it draws on these culturally specific emotional frameworks, producing poems that resonate differently depending on the language chosen. Users who experiment with multiple languages often report discovering entirely new emotional dimensions and expressive possibilities they couldn't access in their mother tongue alone.

Poetic Experiences Beyond Your Mother Tongue

The most exciting possibility that AI multilingual poetry generators open is experiencing the poetic traditions of languages other than your own. When a Korean speaker generates poems on the same theme in English, Japanese, and French, they can feel how each language expresses the world differently. This is a different experience from reading translated literature. Translation is the shadow of the original, but AI-generated poems in each language carry the authentic aesthetics of that language.

UNESCO designates March 21st as World Poetry Day because poetry is the most powerful art form for preserving and celebrating the diversity of languages and cultures. AI & Poem seeks to realize this spirit in the digital age through support for 32 languages. In any language, your emotions and thoughts deserve to become poetry.

Start with your familiar mother tongue, but sometimes venture into the poetic worlds of unfamiliar languages. The journey through multilingual poetry guided by AI will broaden the horizons of your sensibility.
