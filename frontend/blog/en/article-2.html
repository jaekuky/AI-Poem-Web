5 Tips for Writing Poetry Well: Creating with AI

Author: Ryu Jae-kuk | AI & Poem Editorial Team
Published: March 24, 2025 | Last updated: April 5, 2026

by Ryu

Developing AI & Poem and analyzing thousands of AI-generated poems, I discovered that the quality of poetry varies dramatically based on how users approach the creative process. Some users type "write me a sad poem," while others enter "watching the rain from a café window, thinking of someone who left." In my experience, this small difference determines the quality of the result. Here are five principles that I've seen transform ordinary prompts — and ordinary poems — into something remarkable.

Writing poetry is about painting the landscapes of your heart with words. It can sometimes feel overwhelming, but if you remember a few principles, anyone can write great poetry. Whether you use AI tools or pick up a pen yourself, here are 5 tips to elevate your poetry.

1. Observe Specifically

Instead of writing "a beautiful flower," try writing "a red rose kissed by the morning dew." Specific nouns and verbs create vivid images in the reader's mind, rather than abstract adjectives. When inputting a topic into AI, describing specific situations can yield better results.

Having used it directly, I noticed that entering "sad farewell" produces generic imagery, while "a silhouette walking away in the rain" generates far richer, more vivid poetic expression. The more sensory detail you provide, the deeper the AI reaches into its linguistic repertoire.

2. Make the Familiar Strange (Metaphor and Symbolism)

Look at everyday objects from a new perspective. Instead of saying "time flows," express it as "time slips through my fingers like grains of sand." When you make the familiar strange, poetic tension arises.

Analyzing AI & Poem's output patterns, I've found that AI already knows thousands of metaphor structures from its training data. But the most striking metaphors emerge when users combine unexpected domains — like "sweeping memories with a broom." These novel combinations push the AI toward more creative and surprising directions.

3. Emphasize Rhythm

Poetry is a genre that is read with the eyes and heard with the ears. The rhythm that flows smoothly when read aloud is important. Adjust the length of sentences or repeat similar sounds to create a sense of rhythm. Utilizing the 'recital' feature of AI & Poem to check the rhythm of generated poetry is also a great method.

In my experience, listening to a poem through text-to-speech reveals awkward rhythms that are invisible when reading silently. Users who regularly use AI & Poem's audio playback feature consistently produce more polished final poems. The ear catches what the eye misses.

4. Capture Honest Emotions

Raw sincerity resonates more deeply than elaborate embellishments. Be honest about your sadness or joy. When you infuse your experiences and emotions, readers will truly empathize.

Looking at AI & Poem usage data, there's a fascinating pattern: poems generated from abstract requests like "a beautiful love poem" resonate less with users than those from specific emotional situations like "the regret of never confessing to a first love." AI cannot feel emotions, but it follows the direction of authentic feelings that users provide, creating surprisingly delicate expressions.

5. Revision is Essential

You can't be full after the first bite. Hemingway said, "All first drafts are garbage." Instead of using the poem generated by AI as is, change words and rearrange them to refine your work. The more you go through the revision process, the more your poem will shine like a gem.

Having used it directly, I can confirm that poems where users revise the AI's first draft are consistently superior. AI provides structure and direction; humans add their unique experience and subtle sensibility. This collaborative editing process yields the best results. In fact, users who frequently use AI & Poem's editing features report significantly higher satisfaction with their final poems.

Beyond Prompts: Cultivating Poetic Sensibility

I'm convinced that writing better poetry in the AI era isn't about mastering prompt engineering — it's about training your poetic sensibility. These five tips aren't techniques for getting more out of a tool; they're exercises in seeing the world through a poet's eyes. AI amplifies our sensitivity like a speaker amplifies sound, but the music itself can only come from within us. Start by finding one line of poetry in today's landscape. AI & Poem will be here to help shape it into something beautiful.
