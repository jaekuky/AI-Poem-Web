Who Owns AI-Written Poetry? Copyright and Ethical Issues

Author: Jaekuk Ryu | AI & Poem Editorial Team
Published: April 2, 2026 | Last Updated: April 2, 2026

Running AI & Poem, there is one question I receive repeatedly from users: "Can I post the AI-generated poem on my social media?" "Can I compile AI poems into a poetry book?" "Is it okay to put my name as the author of an AI-written poem?" These questions all converge on one fundamental issue — who owns the rights to content created by AI?

This is not merely a legal matter. It is a philosophical question about the essence of creativity, uniquely human expressive abilities, and the boundary between technology and art. When drafting AI & Poem's terms of service, I had to grapple deeply with this issue. Establishing fair standards that protect user rights while reflecting the unique nature of AI technology was no easy task. In this article, we examine the current legal landscape of AI poetry copyright, policy differences across major countries, training data and original author rights, and AI & Poem's approach.

Copyright of AI-Generated Works: Current Legal Landscape

In most countries today, copyright presupposes "human creative activity." The U.S. Copyright Office (USCO) stated clearly in its 2023 guidelines that "content generated independently by AI cannot receive copyright protection." The core principle of copyright law is that the "author" must be a natural person — a human being.

However, this does not mean AI-generated poetry exists entirely outside the realm of copyright. The USCO indicated that for works created through human-AI collaboration, copyright may be recognized for portions where a human exercised "sufficient creative control." In other words, if a human substantially modifies or edits an AI-generated draft, the modified portions may be eligible for copyright.

Applying this to AI & Poem raises interesting questions. When a user enters "heartbreak" as a topic and AI generates a poem, does the user's contribution (selecting the topic) constitute "sufficient creative control"? Most legal experts say topic input alone is insufficient. However, if the user substantially revises the generated poem, or selects and edits results from multiple generations to create a new work, copyright may arise in that final product.

International AI Copyright Policies Compared

United States: Adhering to the Human Author Principle

The United States takes the clearest position. Content generated solely by AI without human creative contribution is not granted copyright. In the 2023 case of comic artist Kristina Kashtanova's AI-illustrated comic "Zarya of the Dawn," the Copyright Office recognized copyright for the human-written text but denied it for AI-generated images. This precedent applies equally to AI poetry.

European Union: The AI Act and Transparency Requirements

The EU, through its AI Act enacted in 2024, strengthened transparency obligations for AI-generated content. Content generated by AI must be labeled as such, with stricter requirements for deepfakes and synthetic media. Regarding copyright itself, the EU generally defers to existing member state laws, but maintains the human author principle overall.

South Korea: Under Discussion

Korean copyright law defines an author as "one who creates a work," which under current law is interpreted as a natural person. While no separate legal provisions for AI-generated works have been established yet, discussions are underway at the Ministry of Culture and the Korea Copyright Commission. Some legal scholars have proposed new rights frameworks akin to "neighboring rights" for AI-generated content.

Japan: A Relatively Flexible Approach

Japan has a comparatively permissive legal environment for AI learning. Under Article 30-4 of its Copyright Act, data use for AI training is broadly permitted. However, regarding copyright of AI outputs, Japan maintains a position similar to the U.S. — protection is possible only when there is "human creative involvement."

The AI Poet's Dilemma: Training Data and Original Authors' Rights

There is another dimension to AI poetry's copyright problem. The training data AI uses to generate poetry — hundreds of millions of existing poems and literary works — raises questions about the original authors' rights. AI learns poetic patterns from human poets' works. As explored in The Future of AI Poetry Generators: Will They Replace Writers?, this reminds us of the fundamental fact that "AI stands on the foundation of human creative works."

Legal judgments on this issue vary by country. In the United States, the New York Times lawsuit against OpenAI is ongoing, and its ruling is expected to set a precedent for the legal status of AI training data. Meanwhile, some legal scholars argue that AI learning is analogous to human "reading." Just as human poets read other poets' works, absorb influences, and write their own verse, AI extracts patterns from training data to create new works.

But the counterargument is equally compelling. There are fundamental differences in scale and method between human reading and AI learning. A human poet reads at most tens of thousands of poems in a lifetime; AI processes hundreds of millions in an instant. Moreover, while humans create unique expressions filtered through their own experiences and emotions, AI lacks any such experiential filter.

AI & Poem's Approach: Transparency and User Rights

Within this complex legal and ethical landscape, AI & Poem has adopted the following principles.

Transparency Principle

All poems generated on AI & Poem are clearly identified as AI-generated content. When users share these poems externally, we encourage disclosure that they are AI-generated. This goes beyond legal obligation — it is AI & Poem's philosophical choice to contribute to establishing ethical standards for the AI era.

Free User Use Principle

Poems generated on AI & Poem may be freely used by users — for personal purposes, social media sharing, greeting cards, and more without restriction. However, for commercial publication of generated poems as-is, our terms of service recommend disclosing their AI-generated origin.

Ongoing Ethical Review

Laws and social consensus regarding AI copyright are evolving rapidly. AI & Poem continuously monitors these changes and updates its service policies accordingly. As discussed in AI Poetry and Emotional Healing, AI poetry is closely connected to users' emotions, making human consideration — not just technical and legal judgment — an important element of policy design.

Finding Balance for the Future of Creativity

The copyright question of AI poetry remains an evolving issue without definitive answers. But the direction of the discussion is becoming increasingly clear: it is the human creative contribution in the process of using AI, rather than the AI output itself, that will become the standard for copyright.

This means the future of AI poetry is heading not toward a "human vs. machine" confrontation but toward "human-machine collaboration." A model where AI generates drafts and humans select, revise, and edit them into final works. In this collaborative model, human aesthetic judgment, emotional depth, and personal experience remain irreplaceable core elements.

Ultimately, what matters may be a more fundamental question than legal ownership. The value of writing poetry lies not in owning the result but in the process of writing itself. No matter how beautiful the poems AI generates, only humans can read them, be moved by them, and empathize with them. And the journey of using AI poetry as a starting point to find your own expression — that is a purely human realm of creativity that no copyright law can protect or regulate.
